DashboardsWhat's included

What a reporting project includes.

Most projects need all of it. Some only need a few parts.

Metric definitions

Agreeing what each number means, before building anything.

  • A written definition for every KPI
  • One owner per metric
  • Edge cases decided up front: refunds, cancellations, returns
  • A glossary your team can actually read

Reporting data model

Clean tables built for reporting, not raw exports.

  • On your warehouse, or a small reporting database we set up
  • Tested and documented
  • History kept for trends
  • Joins across your systems

Management dashboards

The few views leadership checks every week.

  • The handful of numbers that drive your business
  • Trends and comparisons
  • Drill-down from total to detail
  • Readable on a laptop and a phone

Team dashboards

Views built around how each team works.

  • Sales, finance, operations and marketing
  • Filters that make sense to the team
  • Targets and thresholds
  • Self-serve exploration where it helps

Scheduled reports

Reports that arrive on their own.

  • Email delivery on a schedule
  • PDF or spreadsheet exports
  • Different reports for different people
  • No manual refresh

Alerts

A message when a number crosses a line, not a dashboard to stare at.

  • Thresholds you choose
  • Alerts by email or chat
  • Daily or weekly summaries
  • Links straight to the detail

Access & security

Each person sees what they should.

  • Role-based access
  • Row-level filters by team or region
  • Single sign-on where your tool supports it
  • Access reviewed at handover

Speed & cost

Fast pages without an expensive setup.

  • Pre-aggregated tables
  • Sensible refresh schedules
  • Licences sized to who actually uses them
  • Query tuning

Handover & training

Your team can change it without us.

  • Documentation for every dashboard
  • Training for the people who’ll use it
  • Full access to everything we build
  • Support after launch, if you want it

DashboardsIs it right for you?

Honest about fit.

A good fit if…

  • Different teams report different numbers for the same thing
  • Someone rebuilds the same Excel report every week
  • Leadership wants a weekly view without asking an analyst
  • You already have a warehouse, or you’re building one

Probably not yet, if…

Not sure which side you’re on? Ask on the call. We’ll tell you straight.

DashboardsHow it runs

How a reporting project runs.

The same five steps as every project, shaped for this work.

StepWhat happensWhat you get
  1. 1Scope

    What happens: A free call about the decisions you make each week and the reports you use today. Then a written proposal.

    What you get: Scope, timeline and cost in writing, before any work starts.

  2. 2Define

    What happens: We agree what every metric means, who owns it, and which views each team needs.

    What you get: A metric glossary and screen sketches you sign off.

  3. 3Build

    What happens: Data model and dashboards built in short cycles, with a working demo every week.

    What you get: Real dashboards on your data, every week.

  4. 4Validate & launch

    What happens: Numbers checked against your source systems and existing reports, and any differences explained before launch.

    What you get: Tested dashboards, documentation and training.

  5. 5Support

    What happens: We stay on for fixes, changes and the next phase.

    What you get: A team that already knows your numbers.

DashboardsQuestions

Questions, answered.

Which dashboard tool should we use?+
The one you already pay for, if it does the job. If you’re starting fresh, we’ll recommend the simplest option for your size and budget, and explain why in the proposal.
Our numbers don’t match between systems. Can you fix that?+
Usually, yes — that’s most of the work. We trace each number back to its source, agree one definition with you, and build every report on it.
Can we change the dashboards ourselves later?+
Yes. You get full access, documentation and training, so your team can add and edit views without us.
Do we need a data warehouse first?+
Not always. With a few sources, dashboards can sit on a small reporting database we set up. If your data is spread across many systems, we’ll suggest building the data layer first.
Can reports be emailed automatically?+
Yes. Most tools can send scheduled reports by email, and where they can’t, we set up a scheduled job that does.
How long does it take?+
It depends on how many sources and dashboards you need. You get a timeline in writing after the scoping call, and a working demo every week.
Who owns the work?+
You do. Everything runs in your accounts, the code lives in your repository, and you get documentation and a handover session.
